<Chapter title="Mechanics" tone="white" lede="Three axes stacked under a sample. The camera never moves.">

<Split ratio="5/7" sticky>
<div class="prose">

### X / focus sweep
Two MGN12 rails with four MGN12H carriages and an **SFU1605 ball screw** (Ø16, 5 mm lead, 300 mm), direct-driven by a NEMA 17. Nominal resolution is **1.5625 µm** (640 µsteps/mm). Torque and critical speed were sized by calculation.

### Tilt / capstan drive
The cradle turns on two **preloaded 32906X tapered roller bearings**, driven by a capstan with 45 mm and 120 mm drums. The strap is **1.0 mm PETG, 3D-printed** as a single extrusion line and anchored at both ends with an adjustable tensioner. TPU was tested first and discarded: too elastic.

### Pan / turntable
NEMA 11 through a **GT2 belt at 20:60**, on a shaft with two bearings. Nominal resolution is 0.0375°.

### Made in my workshop
PET-CF structural parts, PETG strap, ABS coupling, brass heat-set inserts, CNC-machined MDF base.

<div class="prose">

Fujifilm X-H2 (40 MP) with a Laowa 65 mm f/2.8 2× Ultra Macro APO, on a fixed tripod. Typical capture is f/4 at 1/20 s with the electronic shutter.

The light is a **custom cross-polarised hexagonal LED ring**: six COB strips in a printed holder, linear polariser film on the light and a circular polariser on the lens, PWM-dimmed at 25 kHz from the controller board.

<Chapter title="Firmware" tone="white" lede="An unattended machine has to fail safely. Most of the firmware exists for that.">

<Split ratio="7/5" align="start">
<Figure src="robot/electronics.jpg" alt="Controller board and wiring" ratio="4/3" brief="Octopus Pro and wiring, tidied up, shot top-down." caption="BTT Octopus Pro with three TMC2209 drivers in UART mode." />
<div class="prose">

### Motion
C++17 on STM32duino, about 3,200 lines, on an STM32F429. One hardware timer per axis at 2 MHz, trapezoidal ramps up to 40,000 steps/s, step jitter of ±0.45 µs.

### Link
USB CDC JSON protocol with 20 Hz telemetry.

### Safety
1 s heartbeat, jog lease, IWDG watchdog, driver health checks over UART, software limits, e-stop, and EEPROM config protected by CRC-32.

### Homing
StallGuard4 sensorless homing on X with an adaptive threshold: **σ = 28 µm** over 5 runs (range 65.6 µm). Those are commanded positions, not an independent measurement. StallGuard did not work on tilt, where the signal is too noisy.

### Camera control with no SDK
A C# worker speaks **PTP over Windows WPD**: capture, download, and live view at 1024 × 768. Card-only mode cuts each photo from about 1.5 s to about 0.5 s.

### Dimensioning mode
Mark the front and back of the sample in live view from several angles. The software builds a convex envelope and works out the X range each station needs, so no empty slices are captured.

### Calibration
An automatic 7 × 5 ArUco pose grid feeds one least-squares fit of camera pose, axis zeros and scales, pan eccentricity and per-axis backlash. Modelling the backlash cut reprojection RMS from **5.76 to 1.67 px**. It also found a pan scale of 1.0132, the X rail 8.45° off the optical axis and the pan axis tilted by 3.3°.

**OpenFocus** is my own GPU stacking engine in PyTorch, modelled on the methods of Helicon Focus. It takes about 25 s per stack of 31 × 40 MP images on an RTX 5070 Ti.

There is also an OpenCV engine using ECC registration and Laplacian-pyramid fusion. Helicon Focus itself runs from the command line as a reference for comparison.

### The experiment
I built two pipelines and ran them on the same scan. One trusted the positioner's kinematics for its poses. The other estimated poses from the images alone.

The result was clear: assembly errors (backlash, flexing, the unresolved tilt ratio) are larger than reconstruction can tolerate. So image-based poses became the default, and kinematics are now used for planning and as a prior.

</div>
