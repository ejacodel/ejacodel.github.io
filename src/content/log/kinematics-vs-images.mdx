The obvious idea with a robot scanner is to use its kinematics. You commanded the axes, so you know where every photo was taken. Why estimate what you already know?

So I built that pipeline: poses from the kinematic model, with a bundle adjustment restricted to the 15 parameters of the assembly. Then I built a second one that estimates poses from the stacked images with structure from motion, and uses the kinematics only to choose which neighbouring views to match.

On the same scan, dense-pair reprojection error went from **4.8 px to 0.35 px**, and triangulated RoMa matches went from **26 % to 98 %**.

## What it means

The kinematics aren't bad. The calibration explains a lot of the error: backlash, a pan scale of 1.0132, an X rail 8.45° off the optical axis. But assembly errors on a printed machine are still larger than reconstruction can tolerate at this scale.

So the machine plans the scan and the images fix the poses. Kinematics remain useful as a prior and for planning. They just aren't the final answer.
